## Durable Conclusions

### One time map

A stretch renderer owns one monotonic source-to-output map. Independent
full-band branches cannot be aligned after rendering without blur, doubled
attacks, or micro-echo.

### Complete-system judgment

Identity, perfect reconstruction, exact frame bounds, coefficient consistency,
or a passing synthetic mechanism does not establish professional sound quality.
A candidate must run as one complete renderer before narrower mechanisms are
tuned.

### Transients are structural

Transient placement, phase treatment, replica prevention, and boundary
continuity must be coordinated with the synthesis timeline. Post-render tail
or attack edits did not provide a general solution.

### Resolution is simultaneous

Material-dependent resolution cannot be treated as a later selector bolted onto
independent complete renders. Scale ownership, timing, phase, and synthesis
must be designed together.

### Stereo decisions are shared

Linked stereo needs shared material, reset, peak, trajectory, and transition
decisions while preserving per-channel magnitude and inter-channel relation.
Independent mono engines or late phase projection are insufficient.

### Professional comparison is mandatory

Synthetic controls and objective metrics are rejection gates. Level-matched
long-form listening against a professional reference is the promotion gate.
External source can inform architecture under a clean-room boundary but cannot
be copied or linked as production DSP.

## Rejected Families

- structural branch hybrids
- adaptive transient timelines and fixed-map peak resets
- H/R/P additive rendering
- whole-band phase-gradient integration
- common-grid and frequency-adaptive frames
- independent redundant phase fields
- late linked-stereo projection and peak repair
- coefficient-only material completion as an execution direction

These families may be cited as failure evidence. They must not be restored as
hidden APIs, report modes, or incremental roadmap batches.
